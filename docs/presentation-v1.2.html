No Crumbs

01 · The problem

A folder sorted by when, when the question is always where.

People save videos of places constantly. Saving is instant; getting one back is not. The folder is ordered by the moment you tapped it, so a bakery you saved in March sits hundreds of videos below the day you are standing outside it.

The realistic outcome is not a slow search. It is no search at all.

Dinner somewhere you never saved. That is the loss — not storage, and not elapsed seconds, but a decision made without the thing you deliberately kept.

Ordered byTime. Never by where you are.
Findable byThe caption's words — not a place's name, and not its position on a map.
LastsOnly as long as the account and the video do.

02 · The solution

One link, one confirmation. The app does the rest.

The exchange

You giveOne link, and a tap to confirm.
You getA permanent, private, location-indexed library of places you chose — back in seconds, each keeping the video that recommended it.
Get it inThe app reads the video. Any link shape, nothing tidied up first — and a caption naming no place is a screen of its own, not a failure.
Decide & keepNothing is saved until you confirm it. One place is one pin however many videos name it, and the link back can never be lost.
Get it backYour map is the home surface. Three zoom tiers, a list that always agrees with the pins, and a sentence that narrows what is there without inventing anything.

03 · Architecture

Four layers, one direction, no way back up.

A single Next.js application. The domain layer has no outgoing edge — it cannot reach the network or the database, so the rules stay testable on their own.

04 · Security

Your map is private because the data refuses to be read.

Not because each screen remembers to check. Isolation is held in Postgres, on every table, under a policy forced even for the table's owner — so a route that forgets its filter returns nothing rather than everything.

The keyThe privileged database key never reaches the browser. Its module is server-only, its name carries no public prefix so it cannot be inlined into a bundle, and all eight callers identify the person first.
SharingPeople you share with see the places. Not your note, your tags, your Been marks, or the video you added it from.

On text we did not write

A caption is words to read, never instructions to follow.

The words belong to somebody else, and they are handled that way at every boundary they cross.

05 · Scalability

The ceiling is a quota, not the database.

Load grows with adds, not with accounts. Every read is scoped to one person at the index, so a hundredth account adds rows no query walks. What runs out first is a third-party allowance, and that is a billing decision.

Cost axisOne add is three outside calls. Account count is not the axis.
No queueBy decision. No job outlives its request, and the four tables a queue would have needed already exist as caches inside the source of truth.
Your mapRead once, whole. The drawing is bounded, not the reading — so the list, the pins and search can never disagree.

Measured, not estimated

2,000 pins19.0 ms median, 60.5 ms p95 with labels tiered by zoom. Forced on, the same 2,000 cost 34.0 ms — about 29 fps.
LandingEight waves, 60 ms apart. 480 ms whatever the library size, from the middle outward.
Next limitA hundred lookups a day. Raise it, and cap each person in the same change.

None of that is “the database at hundreds of people”. The architecture is not what limits this product today.

06 · Testing

Every control is proved by making it fail first.

A policy test that only ever passes proves nothing — it may be asserting against an empty table. Each one here denies before it allows, so green means the rule fired, not that nothing happened.

Level 1Unit and domain — 250 files. No network, no database.
Level 2Component render — what a person actually sees.
Level 3In a browser — 13 specifications, at phone and desktop width.
Level 4Database policy — 8 suites, 277 assertions, two real people and the attempt by one to read the other.
Level 5Written procedure — for what a suite honestly cannot judge.

One test, and why it exists

On your map, colour is the whole encoding. So a test asserts two categories stay apart.

Restaurant Café Bar Not known yet

The café amber first drawn sat 1.3 lightness steps from the restaurant persimmon — indistinguishable under deuteranopia, about one man in twelve. The shipped step is 19.0 apart. The test fails the drawn pair.

07 · What comes next

In order, and for a stated reason.

Not a wish list. Each item is the answer to a limit already measured, sequenced by the condition that triggers it.

FirstRaise the lookup quota and cap each person daily, together — so one account cannot spend everyone's allowance.
ThenSet a maximum duration, and build the streaming route that was specified and cut — turning the longest wait in the product into visible progress.
At scalePagination and a viewport read, once a library passes a few hundred places, keeping the bounds the camera needs.
CleanupCount collections instead of loading them, add the one missing index, drop the one that earns nothing.

The rule every one of them is held to

No change may increase what the product asserts.

The failure mode is not an ugly product. It is a beautiful one that fakes progress it cannot measure, and makes the most common outcome feel like a bug.
