The engine reads one sentence and hopes it names a restaurant.

Three quarters of the time it doesn't. That is not a bug anyone can fix in the prompt — it is the whole architecture, which has exactly one input, one model call and one attempt. Engine 2.0 replaces the funnel with a ladder of signals, spends money only when a cheap signal says spending would pay, and keeps every piece of evidence it collected so a place can say where it came from.

01 · What one signal buys

The 27% is a ceiling, not a defect.

Creators put the venue name on screen and say it out loud, because that is what the feed rewards. They put hashtags in the caption, because that is what search rewards. We read the caption. The gap is structural, and it was measured a week ago against sixteen real posts.

3SufficientThe caption names a findable place. Escalating past the caption is pure waste.
5RecoverableA real recommendation whose place is on screen or spoken — never written down.
3Futile“Comment for the name.” The place is in neither the caption nor the video.
5Not a placeCats, an app promo, comedy. Correct answer: nothing, cheaply.
The arithmetic that decides the whole design

A perfect engine that reads everything inside the video moves this sample from 3 posts to 8. It cannot reach 11, because 3 of the posts genuinely do not contain the answer anywhere. So the prize is real and it is bounded: roughly a 2.7× lift, and the design's job is to capture it without paying for the 8 posts where escalating finds nothing.

02 · The critique

Seven things wrong with Engine 1.0, in the order they cost us places.

None of these is sloppiness. Every one of them is a reasonable decision that has since been outgrown by what we now know about the corpus and about what inference costs.

C1

It is a funnel where it should be a ledger.

Caption → candidates → matches → done. Each stage discards what the previous one knew. There is no place to put a fact that arrived from somewhere other than the caption, so there is nowhere to put a second signal even if we had one. The architecture forecloses its own improvement.

C2

Nothing decides whether an add is worth spending on.

Roughly 73% of adds return nothing and most of them should. The best trigger anyone has measured for telling “nothing here” from “everything is in the video” scores 0.33 precision — three wasted escalations for every useful one, which is the difference between a cheap cascade and an unexplainable bill. A cat video and a genuine recommendation whose venue is spoken aloud look identical to every signal the engine currently has.

This one was drafted wrong and the research corrected it. The fix here was going to be the cover frame — those title cards do announce what the video contains. But the same job is done by one extra field on the call we already make, for a fortieth of a cent and no image at all, and the cover frame's own measured recall for venue names is 1 in 8 with false positives. The cheap answer won.

C3

It assumes a name exists. The hard cases have a description instead.

“The best coffee in Tel Aviv is only 9 shekels?! They also have some of the best pizza…” contains no name and four usable facts: a city, a category, a price, and a second category. The schema has no way to express “I know what kind of place this is, not what it is called,” so all four are thrown away — and Google's own text search takes exactly that query.

C4

The matcher never talks back.

If the model proposes a name and no such place exists in that city, that is strong evidence the model misread the caption — and it is discarded. One round trip in the other direction (re-read with “nothing in Tel Aviv is called that”) is the cheapest accuracy we are not buying.

C5

Confidence is self-reported, and we already proved it lies.

A hashtag became a place at 0.95 confidence. Meanwhile the thing that is computable — how many independent signals agree, and whether they agree geographically — is not computed, because with one signal there is nothing to corroborate against.

C6

The person holding the phone is the best sensor in the system and the architecture ignores them.

They watched the video. They read the comment where the creator gave the name. They know which of two branches they meant. Today the only thing we accept from them is a link, and everything they know is discarded before the model is even called.

C7

We already spend more instructing the model than it would cost to let it watch the film.

Reading an entire 60-second video — every frame at one per second, plus the whole audio track — costs the model 5,880 tokens. Our own system prompt costs 5,260 tokens, on every single add, including the roughly three quarters that find nothing at all. Those two numbers are within 12% of each other, and only one of them is buying information.

That is the whole finding, and it is smaller and more useful than the one this page carried on its first two publications. Watching the video is not cheaper than reading the caption — it is 1.8× dearer on the same model, and cutting the prompt widens that to 2.7×. What is true is that the prompt is the single largest line item in the product and it is not information. Fix that before arguing about tiers.

03 · The architecture

Five rungs, climbed only while a cheaper rung says the next one will pay.

Every rung declares what it costs, what has to be true before it runs, and what would make it stop. Nothing escalates on a hunch, and nothing escalates twice. Tap a rung to open it.

04 · Acquisition

The transcript is a real TikTok field. It is behind a door marked “not for commercial products.”

Re-established from primary sources on 2026-08-31, with nine live calls. Full report: docs/evidence/tiktok/08-engine2-access-surface-2026-08-31.md.

The finding that shapes the whole ladder

TikTok's Research API has a field called voice_to_text — “voice to text and subtitles” — and an endpoint that returns a post's comments. Its own FAQ answers “I am a creator, advertiser, or commercial user. Am I eligible?” with the single word “No.” Everything Engine 2.0 wants is real, documented, free, and not for us. The player renders auto-captions on the user's screen and its message API returns numbers and booleans — never text.

Nothing in this document proposes a scraper, a headless browser, a signed-URL mutation or a vendor who does any of those for money. A ban from the one endpoint that works would end the product, and a ToS breach is indefensible in a viva.

The one path that is even arguable, stated without softening

Why it is different in kind

Every prohibition in the terms is about us running an automated system against the platform. If the person taps TikTok's own save button — a function TikTok provides, in TikTok's app, subject to the creator's own download setting — and then picks that file in our app, no automated extraction happened on our side at all. And the US terms affirmatively grant every user a licence to download other users' content.

Two things must not be glossed. The European wording is narrower — “using the Platform for entertainment purposes” — and machine transcription is a poor fit for that phrase. And where a creator has turned downloads off, the licence does not arise and the user cannot get the file anyway, so the product degrades honestly rather than silently.

What it costs the person, which is the real objection

Leave our app, save the video in TikTok, come back, pick the file. Four manual steps to rescue a post whose caption already failed. The specialist's honest guess, labelled assumed: most people will not, and the ones who would will more happily just type the restaurant's name — which is exactly why T2 sits below T3 on the ladder and not above it.

Build the file field around a plain file input, not the share sheet: iOS cannot register a web app as a share target and will not on our timeline. A file input works on every platform for one extra tap.

Three free wins that need nobody's permission

1. Read the markup we are already given. In the caption a hashtag is just characters; in the html blob it is a tagged anchor TikTok itself labelled. The post that turned #tsukijifishmarket into a place at 0.95 confidence has 30 hashtag anchors and 134 characters of prose. “A candidate evidenced only by a hashtag is not a place” becomes checkable against TikTok's own markup instead of a regex we wrote. This removes a confidently wrong place, which this project ranks above every other failure.

2. The cover image is already 1080×1920 — the thumbnail_width field understates the asset by 1.875×. All cover-frame work is unblocked today at full resolution, at zero access cost. Nobody needs to go near a signed URL.

3. A field on the no-places screen that accepts what the person already knows. It is the only mechanism in the entire access report that reaches the posts where the answer exists in neither the caption nor the video. It costs one text field.

The headline, on its third and final version

Switching model pays for the entire video tier and leaves change. Today's caption-only add costs $0.006640. The same caption on the cheaper model costs $0.002192, and reading the whole video on it costs $0.003956 — still 1.7× cheaper than what we pay today for one sentence.

So the answer to “will this cost ten to thirty times more” is still no — but the reason is not that video is cheap. Video is the most expensive rung and always was. The reason is that we are currently overpaying for text by a factor of three, and paying it on a prompt that is four fifths of the bill.

Three corrections, printed rather than swapped

This page said ten times, then 3.8 times, and both were wrong. The first compared against a cheaper model than the one we have configured. The second still compared a marginal cost — the video tokens alone — against a whole call, which is the classic way to make a cost headline say something untrue. On one model id, like for like, watching the video is 1.8× dearer than reading the caption, and cutting the prompt widens that to 2.7× rather than inverting it. The claim is retracted; the table above is the corrected one.

Third: an earlier row claimed prompt caching takes us to 0.59×. It is deleted, and the verdict is no. At 200 adds a month caching costs money at both expiry settings — you pay a 25–100% write premium on essentially every call because one add every four days never finds a warm cache. It only pays at a one-hour expiry above roughly a thousand adds a month, and even at three thousand it saves about $13.71 — less than cutting the prompt, and less than switching model, both of which work at every volume and neither of which can backfire.

And the ladder does not add up the way ladders look like they do

Every rung above the caption is chasing the same five posts — the ones whose venue is on screen or spoken. They are not five independent chances at five different posts. A perfect escalator takes the corpus from 27% to 73%; realistically it is nearer 55%, and the three posts where the answer exists nowhere in the video are unreachable at any price by any rung except asking the person. Read the switches as overlapping routes to one pool, not as a stack of gains.

What the research killed — including two of my own ideas

Compressing the audio saves nothing

This was in the brief and it is wrong, which is worth being blunt about. Every transcription service bills by seconds of audio, not by bytes — and the one that bills by tokens charges a flat 32 per second regardless of the file. Compressing a 60-second clip from 874 KB to 113 KB was measured, and it saves $0.00 while costing fifteen times the processing. Copying the audio track out untouched takes 0.06 s; re-encoding it takes 0.90 s. Speeding the audio up does cut the bill, because duration is what is billed — and it raises the error rate from about 18% to about 26% to save three hundredths of a cent.

Dedicated text recognition loses on both axes

A purpose-built recognition service costs $0.0015 per image. A general model reads the same frame for $0.000155 — ten times less — and does the place extraction in the same call rather than handing back a bag of words. Our own cover-frame run already measured what a bag of words is worth here: it offered “Rama Hair & Be” and “Ersimes Organic & Her” — two Brixton shopfronts — as restaurant recommendations.

Running our own transcription loses too

A rented GPU costs about 1.5× the hosted price before you count the cold start — and the cold start is 12 to 90 seconds against a stage budget of 12. A cold container eats the entire add. The single argument that survives is a Hebrew-specific model, and that is a quality argument rather than a cost one. See below, because it may be the one that matters.

And the two ideas of mine that did not survive

The cover frame as the escalation trigger — beaten by a field on a call we already make, for a twentieth of the price and no image. And splitting frames from audio as two rungs — reading the whole video costs half of what doing those two things separately costs, so the split only earns its place for latency and for corroboration, not for money.

The risk that is not about money

Nobody publishes a Hebrew accuracy figure for speech over a music bed at TikTok pacing, which is exactly our corpus. The one vendor band that exists places Hebrew at 10–25% word error. And word error is the wrong measure for us anyway: we need one proper noun right. An error rate concentrated in ordinary words is survivable; the same rate that turns Simhovich into Simchovic is a total loss on the only token that mattered. No public benchmark measures that. There is an open Hebrew-tuned transcription model that would be the answer if this proves to be the blocker — and measuring it on our own audio is a task, not a citation.

Read the switches above with this in mind

T1's price is the whole call; every rung above it is marginal — what it adds on an add that reaches it. The blended figure applies each rung's reach, and the video rung is additionally scaled by how often a person supplies a file at all.

Two different unmeasured numbers are doing a lot of work here, and they are not the same number. One is how often we escalate past the caption — assumed at 50%, against a measured 73% of adds that find nothing, on the bet that the verdict field suppresses the rest. The other is how often the person actually hands us a video — assumed at 45%, and that one is a guess about human behaviour, not about software. If a reader conflates them the whole model reads wrong.

And with every switch on, the yield above reads near 71% — which is essentially the perfect-escalator ceiling. That is what the ladder is worth if every rung works. No rung above the caption has ever been run. Treat the top of this dial as the best case it is, which is exactly why the first task in the build order is a harness rather than a feature.

06 · The data structure

A place stops being a guess and becomes a stack of receipts.

One structure carries the whole redesign. Every fact a rung produces is appended with where it came from, what was literally said, and when in the video it was said — and nothing is ever overwritten by a later, more confident-sounding rung.

Why append-only matters more here than anywhere else

The standing rule in this project is that an uncertain result beats a confidently wrong one. With one signal that rule costs nothing, because there is never a conflict. With five signals conflicts are the normal case: the caption says one thing, the speech says another, the matcher offers a third. A structure that keeps all three, and a score computed from how many agree, is the only version of that rule that survives contact with more evidence.

Corroboration replaces self-reported confidence

Today the model reports its own confidence and we already know it inflates. Corroboration is arithmetic we do ourselves: a name said aloud at 0:14 and burned on screen at 0:14 and matched to one address in the right city is a different object from a name that appeared once in a hashtag — and the difference is computable, explainable and testable.

The bottom outcome already has a home. place_mentions shipped in migration 0031 for exactly this: a place a post named that we could not confirm, kept on the user's own row with the reason it did not become a pin. Engine 2.0 does not need a new table for its honest failures — it needs to stop producing dishonest successes.

07 · After the places

A trip is a collection with two dates on it.

Ruled 2026-08-31. The full ruling is docs/evidence/product/engine2-trip-matching-2026-08-31.md; this is what it decided and what it refused.

No new object, no new noun

collections gains starts_on and ends_on, both nullable. No trips table, no /trips route. A collection is already a private map, already shareable, already ordered, already carries membership rules that were reviewed adversarially before they shipped — a trips table re-buys all of that at full price to get a date range.

The user-facing word stays collection. The dates render on the header as a fact (3–9 Sep) and nowhere else. Making trip a real noun in the product is a change to the vocabulary table, which is the owner's call, not a developer's.

The trap inside that migration

0024's grants are column-level on purpose (grant update (name, description)). Two new columns are therefore not writable from a browser until the migration extends the grants too. Add the columns and stop, and you ship a feature that fails at runtime, not at deploy — silently, and only for real users.

The matching rule, which reuses a predicate rather than inventing one

Three variations on these constants have already been measured and rejected. A fourth needs the same standard of evidence.

Area — the gate

linked(a,b) ⇔ km(a,b) ≤ 2
                ∨ ( km(a,b) ≤ 50 ∧ locality(a) = locality(b) )

Single-link against any member of the collection, never a centroid — a collection spanning London has members 11 km apart. A collection with no places has no area and matches nothing; that is correct, and the manual picker is the recovery.

Dates — re-ranking only, never a gate

Tier A: dated and live (today ∈ [start−14d, end+3d]). Tier B: undated. Tier C: dated and not live. Within a tier: more matched places wins, then most recently touched, then id. Deterministic at every step, because the same library must produce the same suggestion twice.

The 14/3-day window is assumed, and it is contained on purpose: a wrong window can only reorder suggestions, never hide a collection.

Being wrong is the part that decides whether this is loved

The suggestion lands on the review screen, before the save — one control for the whole add, not one per place — and it says its reason (“4 places already in this collection are nearby”). It is pre-ticked only for a private collection with exactly one match. Never pre-ticked into something other people can see. A multi-city post is split by area and offered at most one collection per area; it never creates a collection on its own.

The extra fields for planning — two kept, two cut

And the size of the ask was smaller than it looked: tags, dishes and why_go already ship per save, and have since 0019.

09 · The prerequisite

None of this may be built before we can measure it.

“It doesn't work” is a judgement no number in this repository can currently confirm or refute. That is the actual first defect, and it is the cheapest one to fix.

What is wrong today

  • The corpus is 13 links, and it was assembled from web search, so it over-represents captions that name places — it flatters every number taken on it.
  • Nothing runs in CI. The harness needs live keys and a live database.
  • There is no .env.local in this checkout at all, so it cannot be run here even by hand.
  • Cost was logged as 0 with costModel: "unmeasured" — honest, and useless for a decision about money. That one is now closable: the missing per-token price was found and is two constants.

What replaces it — and it is a day of work

  • Fifty links the owner actually saved, labelled by hand into the four classes, with the true places written down. This is the only sample whose distribution matches the product.
  • Four numbers per run: places found, places wrong, cost, latency — each per class, so a change that helps listicles and hurts single-venue posts cannot hide inside an average.
  • A fixture mode that replays stored captions, frames and transcripts with no network, so the graded run can live in CI and cost nothing.
  • Every rung scored on and off, so the cascade's stop rules are tuned against evidence rather than taste.
Say the quiet part

The hallucination bar in the acceptance criteria is ≤5% while multi-place recall is only ≥60%. Every rung added below raises recall and the chance of a confident wrong place. Without the harness, Engine 2.0 is a way to be wrong faster and at greater expense. Build the harness first. It is not overhead; it is the only thing that makes the rest safe.

A trap laid for T1b, found while checking it

The obvious way to purge a caption is to null the column. That would silently break every future add of that post, permanently. The cache is gated on the fetch status, not on the caption — the adapter says so in its own comment — and the row-to-source mapper turns a null caption into no text at all. So a purged row that still reads “fetched successfully” becomes a cache hit returning nothing, for every user, forever: no network call, no re-fetch, just “this post has no caption” from then on.

A purge has to move the fetch status too, or the gate has to learn about purging. Confirmed by reading both halves and by checking the blast radius: the caption column has exactly one reader in the whole application, which is what makes this cheap to get right and easy to get wrong. It is not a live defect — nothing writes that null today. It is a loaded trap waiting for whoever implements the fix above.

11 · Not mine to decide

Seven decisions that are yours, and one of them costs a tap.

Everything else in this document is an engineering call and has been made. These seven are not, and three of them gate real work. The last one arrived while this was being written and is about what already ships.

D1

May a person hand us their own saved video, for us to transcribe?

This is the only media path that is even arguable, and it gates the top rung of the ladder. It turns on a legal question, not a technical one: the American terms grant every user a licence to download other users' content, and the European terms narrow that to “using the Platform for entertainment purposes” — which machine transcription fits badly. Needs you and security-privacy together. Nothing above rung two starts before you answer.

D2

Will you request one TikTok data export today?

Settings → Account → Download your data. One tap, back within four days, and it converts the bulk-add path from “blog posts say the file looks like this” into something measured. It is the cheapest missing evidence in this entire proposal, and the answer also decides whether the growth plan's queue idea has an input worth building for.

D3

Is this session's work the engine, or the sixth of September?

The product lead's ruling is explicit: file the trip work and build none of it now, because four graded documents do not exist and nothing can land at all while the build runner refuses to start. That is an argument about the calendar and it is correct on its own terms. It is still your call whether the thing you want fixed is the engine or the submission — and E2-T0 through E2-T3 are the rows that are worth doing under either answer.

D4

Does trip become a word the product says?

The ruling keeps collection and renders the dates as a fact on the header, because the vocabulary table forbids a second word for one object. Overriding that is a change to the table, which is yours. My own view: the ruling is right, and “3–9 Sep” under a collection name does the whole job.

D5

What is the ceiling on what one add may cost?

The governor needs a number to enforce, and it is a product decision rather than a technical one. Today an add costs two thirds of a cent and four fifths of that is waste. Give it a ceiling per add and a ceiling per person per month and the design enforces both — and note that on the figures opposite, a ladder that reaches every rung still lands under today's number once the waste is gone.

D6

Do we hold the grounding line, or attribute the model's own knowledge as its own?

Already open in current-state.md, and the ladder makes it urgent rather than theoretical. With five signals the model will frequently know something none of them said. Today that leaks silently into fields labelled what the caption says — measured: falafel and middle eastern arrived as tags on a caption that mentioned neither. The honest options are to drop it or to label it, and the ledger makes labelling cheap for the first time.

D7

A licence and a prohibition, in the same document, about what already ships.

TikTok's Developer terms — a different document from the one this project has been reading — forbid building “profiles, databases, or similar records on any individual, device, content, or browser.” The same agreement, one section earlier, expressly licenses you to “use automated means in your Application to collect information” and to use what you collect “solely for the purpose of developing, maintaining, and supporting your Applications.” Both cannot be read literally, since a flat ban on storing anything would nullify the collection just granted.

Read together, the prohibition targets profiling and bulk dataset-building, which our cache is not — its neighbouring limbs are about personal data and about associating behaviour with an individual or device. We also satisfy the agreement's sharing restriction outright: the stored caption is granted to no application role at all, so it is made available to nobody.

And underneath the arguable thing sits a real one. What keeps the clause debatable is that our cache is shared, global and permanent — and the permanence is not a reading, it is a fact I checked: there is no path anywhere in this codebase by which a stored caption is ever deleted. Not on account deletion, not on a timer, not at all. That is owed under storage limitation and under the account-deletion work regardless of how anyone reads TikTok's terms, and it is the one part of D7 that is a task rather than a question.

The sharper risk is not that clause, and it is worth reading twice. The licence opens “you may, solely as described in the TikTok Developer Documentation…”, and the oEmbed documentation describes exactly one purpose: converting a link into embedded video markup. It does not describe caching a caption and running a model over it. If those words govern the purpose of use rather than the manner of access, then a product whose entire value comes from not embedding the post sits outside the licence — and no amount of retention hygiene fixes that. The specialist reads it as manner-of-access, and calls that the weakest joint in the whole analysis.

How far it is verified, because that decides what you can do with it. The document was captured by a different task and carries no address, no status and no timestamp; a re-fetch hit two dead links and a redirect that returned nothing. What holds it up is internal consistency — the agreement's own cross-references describe the clause by subject matter and all seven of them resolve. Reliable enough to raise the question. Not reliable enough to change production on. Re-fetch it properly, put both paragraphs in front of security-privacy together rather than one instead of the other, and fix the capture so the next raw file records where it came from.

What I would do on Monday if you told me nothing

E2-T0, E2-T1, E2-T2, E2-T3, in that order. The harness, because “it doesn't work” is currently unfalsifiable. The prompt, because it is four fifths of the bill and cutting it carries no behavioural risk at all. The markup parse, because it deletes a confidently wrong place. The verdict field, because everything expensive downstream is guesswork without a trigger and this one costs a fortieth of a cent. None of the four needs a ruling, a new port, or a byte of media — and together they take the bill to roughly a third of today's while raising the yield, before anyone has to argue about transcription.
