## 1. Ranked — what the owner would say next

### 1. `/collections` on a phone is a blank white sheet · **measured** · every library size

Full-screen opaque sheet, the map behind it gone, one collection card and a dashed "New collection"
row, then nothing. Content ends at **y ≈ 240** and the bottom nav floats at **y = 776** on an 844 px
viewport: **≈ 536 px, 63 % of the screen, is empty**. Identical at 0, 3, 30 and 300 places, both
themes.

It is the same at every count because the fixture supplies exactly one collection — see §5.6 — but
one collection *is* the realistic state, collections having just shipped, and the screen has no
design for it. Reproduce: sign in, tap **Collections** at 390×844.

Desktop is the same shape and worse in proportion: the left panel holds two rows and **≈ 87 %** of
its height is empty.

### 2. On desktop `/map`, the filters take more of the panel than the places do · **measured**

Panel budget at 1440×900, top of panel to bottom:

| library | header + CTA + search + chips + sort + tags | the list itself | list content |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3 | 368 px (**41 %**) | 447 px (50 %) | 447 px |
| 30 | 524 px (**58 %**) | **291 px (32 %)** | 3,431 px |
| 300 | 524 px (**58 %**) | **291 px (32 %)** | 34,300 px |

The chrome **grows with the library** — one chip per category plus one per tag — and the list's
window shrinks to pay for it. At 30 places the user's own places get 2.4 rows of a 900 px screen; at
300 they scroll 34,300 px through a 291 px porthole. The main screen of the product gets worse the
more the product is used.

### 3. At 300 places the map stops working, and the whole library is behind a chevron · **looked at, counts measured**

Mobile 390×844, 300 places: the pins pile into one unreadable mound over central Tel Aviv. No
clustering, no aggregation — 300 individual markers, most of them fully covered. No single place can
be picked. The sheet is collapsed to a **≈ 124 px peek** whose entire content is the line
`300 in Tel Aviv-Yafo ˄`; **zero list items are visible** at 0, 3, 30 or 300 places until the sheet
is dragged. Desktop at 300 has the same pin mound in the right two-thirds.

`/import?state=no-places` is `mvp-plan.md`'s modal import outcome, ~73 % of imports. Dominant painted

### 5. The front door scrolls sideways on every phone · **measured** · `/` and `/sign-in`

## 2. What passed, with its limits stated

Recording these so nobody hunts them again, and so the limits are visible.

- **No clipped ink at `9a95444`.** The committed `tests/harness/measure-clipping.mjs`, production
  build, 6 routes × 2 themes × 2 viewports at 12 places: **0 elements with ink cut off**, from 58
  geometric candidate records the pixel half cleared. My own dev-mode sweep extended the geometry half to
  **16 routes × 2 themes × 2 viewports × 4 library sizes** and every additional candidate it raised
  is accounted for in §5.3. The limit: no `/import` screen has been through the *pixel* half.
- **Focus rings are effectively universal.** Driven by a real `Tab` press across nine routes, both
  themes, both viewports: 337 stops, of which **7 sat in an animated region and are reported
  inconclusive rather than passing**, leaving 330 judged — **326 paint a visibly different box and 4
  do not**. Those 4 are two controls (`Zoom in`, `Toggle attribution` on mobile `/collections`) in
  both themes, and they are the buried controls of finding 9: the ring is drawn, under an opaque
  sheet.
- **No console errors, no uncaught exceptions, no failed requests** on any of 128 route × theme ×
  viewport × count combinations. The only entries in the log are the 404 and the 500 this review
  injected on purpose.
- **Layout shift is ~0** on every route except the import screens, which score **0.011–0.033** — well
  inside the good band, and worth knowing only because they are the only non-zero readings.
- **The zero-state camera is correct.** `Asia/Jerusalem` opens on Tel Aviv; `UTC` and
  `America/New_York` behave exactly as `src/ui/place/viewport.ts` documents. London on an unmapped
  zone is the designed fallback, not a bug — which is what it looks like to anyone driving the app
  with a default-timezone browser.
- **Hover is correctly absent on touch.** Every control on a `hasTouch` context reports no hover
  change, which is `@media (hover: hover)` working. My first pass reported this as ~40 defects; see
  §5.2.
- **Everything below the fold is reachable.** On `/import?state=review` and desktop `/map`, every
  control that starts outside the viewport comes into view under `scrollIntoView` via an inner
  scroller.

## 5. Seven instruments that lied in this review, three of them mine

`iteration-2-record.md` §3 is the catalogue this belongs to. Each of these produced a confident wrong
answer, and each was caught by a different mechanism.

**5.1 — an emulated phone is not a phone, and it hid a 28 px defect behind a 1 px number.**
Playwright's `isMobile` context gives a **visual** viewport of 417 px against a **layout** viewport of
390. So 27 of the 28 overflowing pixels are already inside the visual viewport and only **1 px**
remains scrollable. My probe read `maxScrollLeft = 1` and I nearly filed finding 5 as a hairline.
Re-measured in a non-mobile context at the same widths: **28 px at 430, 22 at 500, 2 at 540, 0 at
560.** A real 390 px phone has both viewports at 390 and gets the whole 28.
*Caught by:* the two numbers disagreeing — `scrollWidth − clientWidth = 28` against
`maxScrollLeft = 1` — and refusing to publish either until they agreed.

**5.2 — `element.focus({ focusVisible: true })` does not set `:focus-visible` on a touch context.**
The first states pass reported **12 controls with no focus ring**, including the zoom buttons and the
attribution links on three surfaces. Re-driven with a real `Tab` press, the count is **2 distinct
controls** (4 records across both themes), and both are the occlusion of finding 9 rather than a
missing ring. The same pass reported ~40 hover failures
on mobile, which is `@media (hover: hover)` doing its job.
*Caught by:* the desktop and mobile columns disagreeing for the same control — a defect in a ring
token does not care about `hasTouch`, so the instrument had to.

**5.3 — geometry-only clipping over-reports by an order of magnitude, in the flattering direction.**
Eleven candidates with 39–417 px of "overhang" on desktop `/map` and `/import?state=review`. All of
them are content below the fold of an *inner* scroller whose nearest `overflow: hidden` ancestor is
`main.relative.h-dvh` — the walk correctly steps over `auto`/`scroll` and then finds the page. The
committed pixel half returns **0 device px of lost ink** for every one, and `scrollIntoView` reaches
every below-fold control. This is `iteration-2-record.md` §3.4 rule 1 arriving at a different
ancestor. **Geometry says *whether*; only pixels say *how much*, and only a reachability test says
*whether it matters*.**

**5.4 — a bounding-box overlap is not an overlap.** My detector reported a place row's note text
intersecting the word `Collections` by 225 × 16 px on desktop `/map` at 300. The row's box does
extend past the scroller; its ink is clipped at the edge and is never painted over the footer. The
real defect at that spot is the guillotine of finding 15, which the overlap detector could not see.

**5.5 — every screenshot taken in dev mode carries a defect that is not the product's.** Next's dev
overlay renders a dark `N` disc in the bottom-left corner, directly on top of the mobile bottom nav's
`Map` label, and it is a **tab stop** — it appears 66 times in the raw traversal data. It was on
course to be filed as an overlapping-avatar defect. Every judged capture in this review injects
`nextjs-portal { display: none }` first; the earlier scale captures do not, and their bottom-left
corner should be ignored.

**5.6 — an occlusion probe run at `scrollTop = 0` answers a different question than the reader's.**
The `/profile` sweep found a control and two sentences under the fixed chrome and would have filed
them as buried. Re-run at maximum scroll: **zero occluded elements**, at every count and both
viewport heights. *Is this covered right now* and *can the reader ever see it* are two questions, and
only the second is a defect. Both are reported in §7.2 because the first one is what a person sees on
arrival.

**5.7 — the fixture caps a collection at six items regardless of library size.**
`collectionMemberRows` uses `Math.min(placeCount, 6)`, so `/collections` reads `6 places` at 30 and at
300. That is the harness, not the product, and no finding here rests on a collection's size.

## 6. Separately: the harness was telling the owner they were someone else

Not a product bug, and it was reported as one.

The owner smoke-tested through `tests/harness/serve-local.mjs`, signed in **with their own email**,
and `/profile` showed **`demo@example.com`**. `src/app/profile/page.tsx:92` reads `user.email` off the
real session and is correct; the stub GoTrue accepted any credentials and then answered with its
fixture user regardless of what was typed.

**Fixed in `tests/harness/`, at the identity layer rather than at the one screen.**

- `POST /auth/v1/token` and `/auth/v1/signup` now read the submitted body and thread the email into
  the session they return; `refresh_token` grants carry no email and deliberately do not reset it.
- The minted JWT carries the real address as its `email` claim, and `GET /auth/v1/user` reads that
  claim back — **per request, and authoritative over anything the process remembers**, so a token
  minted for one identity can never be answered with another's, and a session cookie that outlives a
  stub restart still resolves to the right person.
- `POST /auth/v1/logout` returns the stub to its default identity.
- `profileRow` derives `display_name` from the address (`orel@arbitrip.com` → `Orel`) instead of a
  constant `Demo`, because `/profile` prints the name on the line above the email — the same lie, one
  line up. `demo@example.com` still yields exactly `Demo`, so **every screenshot taken before this
  change still renders the same two lines**, and no existing harness changes behaviour.

Fixture *places* are unchanged and stay obviously synthetic. `Sabich Counter No. 1` is deliberate.

**Verified, at `9a95444` with the working-tree harness:** signed in through the real form as
`orel@arbitrip.com`, `/profile` renders `Orel` / `orel@arbitrip.com`, and `demo@example.com` appears
nowhere on the page. Seven protocol cases pass, including the two that matter beyond the happy path —
a token issued to one identity still answers with that identity after a second person signs in, and a
`refresh_token` grant does not reset the identity to the default. Known-answer control: the same
probe against `git show 9a95444:tests/harness/stub-supabase.mjs` returns `demo@example.com` for all
three reads.

## 7. Three follow-ups, taken against `96d5d70`

Requested after the sweep. Different base commit from §1–§5, stated on each.

### 7.1 `/collections/[id]` frames its own collection, not the library · **measured** · `96d5d70`

The shell lane's one unmeasured claim, and it now has a number.

The design of the test matters more than the result. The collection holds the **same six places** at
30 library places and at 300, while the library's own box grows from **3.0×** to **4.5×** the
collection's span. So a camera driven by the collection produces the *same picture* at both counts,
and a camera driven by the library produces a visibly wider one at 300. That makes it a known-answer
test rather than an inspection.

| | result |
|---|---|
| control — two captures at 30 places | **byte-identical** (so the comparison means something) |
| the 920 × 900 map region at 30 vs at 300 | **byte-identical** |
| the six pins' painted extent | 608 × 771 px of that region, at both counts |
| library span ÷ collection span | 3.0× at 30 places, 4.5× at 300 |

**The camera frames the collection.** The claim is no longer reasoned.

**The premise the request was built on is wrong against the tree, and that is worth more than the
result.** `/collections/[id]` does **not** read `collection_items`. It reads `collections` with
`DETAIL_SELECT`, whose `items:collection_items ( … )` **embed** `collectionDetailRows` has supplied
all along — which is why §1 finding 6 and the §1.2 captures already show six labelled pins on that
route at `9a95444`. Confirmed twice over: the route renders its pins, and across a full run the app
**never requested `/rest/v1/collection_items` once**. The only direct readers of that table are the
mutation server actions in `src/app/actions/collections.ts`, which this stub answers no writes for.

So the route has been exercised, and the fixture was never the blocker.

**`collection_items` fixtures were added anyway, and they change nothing.** `collectionItemRows` in
`tests/harness/fixtures.mjs` derives from `collectionDetailRows` rather than restating it, because
two hand-written copies of one shape drift and one of them is always the stale one. They exist so the

### 7.2 `/profile`: it is scroll position, not occlusion — but a control arrives buried · **measured** · `96d5d70`

### 7.3 The flag glyph degrades as documented, and the documentation is slightly wrong · **measured (the glyph), unverified (the fallback)**

`WHERE YOU SAVE` renders a genuine regional-indicator pair — measured, `🇮🇱`, `U+1F1EE U+1F1F1` — and
the `span` is `aria-hidden` beside a row that already names the country, so nothing is lost to a
screen reader. `src/app/profile/page.tsx:195` states the reasoning.

Its last clause is the part to note: *"a platform with no flag glyphs simply draws nothing beside a
row that already names the country."* Chromium and Firefox on Windows do not draw nothing — they draw
the two regional indicators as **boxed capitals**, so the row would read `IL Israel 3` and name the
country twice. **I could not test that**: this review ran on macOS, which has the flags. It is
recorded as the recorded-reasoning-goes-stale shape (`iteration-2-record.md` §5) rather than as a
defect, and it needs one look on a Windows browser to settle.

Note the asymmetry with the map: `country-flag-image.ts` **measures the painted box** of the glyph
before drawing it into the pill and falls back when it cannot. The profile path has no equivalent,
which is defensible — it is decorative there and load-bearing on the pill — but it is the reason the
two surfaces would disagree on a platform without flags.
